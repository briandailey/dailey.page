How do I write this thing? It's hard to get all of the ideas to cohere. What are we working with here?

- The importance of social connections to mental health
- The detrimental effect of isolation
- Offices aren't necessarily the answer
- Proximity is king
- The benefits of a place that emphasizes _neighborhood_
- Ideally, built out in a way that encourages social interactions
    - Still gotta make a point to do this, if possible.
- **What's the conclusion here? What's the goal of this paean?**
    - If you can find a third space that meets these requirements, _leverage it_. It will alleviate some of the isolation; treat it like diet and exercise.

Generally speaking, I'm a fan of remote work. It does, however, have some trade-offs. Specifically, there are some [credible studies](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec7671) that have indicated that it can have a detrimental effect on your mental health, particularly if you are working in completely isolation.

This further exacerbates the already-urgent need for [third places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_place). There is ample evidence that positive human interactions boost well-being, and third places tend to facilitate those interactions.

This brings me to [Switchyards](https://www.switchyards.com/). Switchyards opened their first location out of Atlanta [back in late 2023](https://whatnow.com/nashville/restaurants/neighborhood-work-club-and-coffee-bar-debuts-next-nashville-location/) and given that this new location was within walking distance of my house, I immediately became a member.

I was already familiar with the idea of co-working spaces (I had previously used WeWork and eSpaces in Nashville) so what I expected was just a very convenient place to work outside of my home. However, what I _didn't_ expect was a factor that Switchyards seems to be pretty intentional about. You see, on all their marketing material they describe it as a "_neighborhood work club._". The use of "neighborhood" seems to indicate that they predominately open clubs in, well, neighborhoods, specifically. They aren't typically located in office districts or downtown corridors. Hence, they pull in a lot of people like myself: those that live in the neighborhood. Offices, in contrast, often build in urban cores, are centrally located, pulling people from all over a metropolis. Many co-working spaces have followed that strategy, which meant that you're still doing the same commute, and you're working alongside people that you only ever see while you're working.

> Switchyards is the world’s first neighborhood work club. A place designed for your best thinking and most productive work. *Near home. Around friends and neighbors.* (Pulled from the Switchyards front page, emphasis mine.)

Switchyards took things in a different direction. By pulling a lot of members that are from _my immediate neighborhood_ meant that I suddenly started seeing a lot of familiar faces around the coffee machine. Folks that I saw in the school drop-off line, the local restaurants, my church, etc.

-- Note how I had to be intentional about this.--:x
