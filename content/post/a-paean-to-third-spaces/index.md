Generally speaking, I'm a fan of remote work. It does, however, have some trade-offs. Specifically, there are some [credible studies](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec7671) that have indicated that it can have a [detrimental effect on your mental and physical health](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-soc-031021-012001), particularly if you are working in complete isolation. The clear trend is in the wrong direction, too: generally speaking, people are spending [less time socializing](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/13/americans-neighbors-socializing-less).

This trend further exacerbates the already-urgent need for [third places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_place). There is ample evidence that positive human interactions boost well-being, and third places tend to facilitate those interactions.

This brings me to [Switchyards](https://www.switchyards.com/). Switchyards opened their first location out of Atlanta [back in late 2023](https://whatnow.com/nashville/restaurants/neighborhood-work-club-and-coffee-bar-debuts-next-nashville-location/) and given that this new location was within walking distance of my house, I immediately became a member.

I was already familiar with the idea of co-working spaces (I had previously used WeWork and eSpaces in Nashville) so what I expected was just a very convenient place to work outside of my home. However, what I _didn't_ expect was a factor that Switchyards seems to be pretty intentional about. You see, on all their marketing material they describe it as a "_neighborhood work club._". The use of "neighborhood" seems to indicate that they predominately open clubs in, well, neighborhoods, specifically. They aren't typically located in office districts or downtown corridors. Hence, they pull in a lot of people like myself: those that live in the neighborhood. Offices, in contrast, often build in urban cores, are centrally located, pulling people from all over a metropolis. Many co-working spaces have followed that strategy, which meant that you're still doing the same commute, and you're working alongside people that you only ever see while you're working.

> "Switchyards is the world’s first neighborhood work club. A place designed for your best thinking and most productive work. *Near home. Around friends and neighbors.*"

(This quote is pulled from the Switchyards front page, but the emphasis is mine.)

Switchyards took things in a different direction. By pulling a lot of members that are from _my immediate neighborhood_ meant that I suddenly started seeing a lot of familiar faces around the coffee machine. Folks that I saw in the school drop-off line, the local restaurants, my church, etc. Each Switchyards location has a centralized coffee machine that takes about 70 seconds to brew a ([very good](https://crema-coffee.com/collections/switchyards)) cup of coffee. That's an excellent amount of time to have a short chat. To make room for serendipity, I made a rule for myself: if anyone else is in the coffee area, I can't look at my phone or have it out. This opens up the room for more friendly conversation. Since I've been a member of Switchyards [since their Nashville opening in 2023](https://www.hypepotamus.com/switchyards-and-its-work-club-concept-moves-into-music-city/), it's become a bit like the ["Cheers" theme song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Everybody_Knows_Your_Name): I consistently say hello to two or three fellow members when I drop by to get work done. Those short connections are a huge social and emotional boost!

Furthermore, I've been able to make use of Switchyard's conference rooms to host other group completely unrelated to my work: AI discussion groups, bible studies, game nights with friends. (Sure, I could use my house but I have six kids so it's nice to have an alternative location.)

All this to say: I really appreciate Switchyards. Being a member has improved my life in measurable ways and is easily one of the monthly subscriptions that I easily find to be providing the most value-per-dollar.

Keep it up, Switchyards team. Y'all are awesome.
